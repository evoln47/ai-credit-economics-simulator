Policy levers

These are the choices a pricing team actually owns. Flip any of them and panels 4 and 5 reprice revenue, margin, and breakage on the spot. This is also where the competitor shapes can be replayed: Wix is hard cap with no top-ups, Framer is top-ups with published burn rates.

Credit rollover
Unused credits bank into next month, capped at one month of allowance. Off matches Squarespace today: monthly credits do not roll over.

AI gross margin is (revenue − AI serving cost) ÷ revenue, per user per month, plan price plus any overage or top-up revenue. A tier is flagged when its overall margin or its p99 user's margin falls under this floor.

04

Tier margins in the steady-state month

Expected serving cost is what the plan actually pays out under the policy above — under a hard cap, heavy users cost no more than their allowance; under overage, they cost their full demand and pay for the excess. Margin is per user per month on plan revenue plus overage or top-up revenue.

Simulation: 10,000 users per tier × 6 months, fixed seed so identical settings always give identical figures. Breakage is the share of credits available in the month (grant plus banked rollover) that expire unused. Costs at median / p90 / p99 are read from the simulated users nearest those demand percentiles, not from the fitted curve. One caution on the overage and top-up columns: that revenue is a mechanical upper bound, because it bills the simulated shortfall in full before any upgrade, churn, or spending-cap behaviour — in a live product the heaviest users upgrade or leave rather than pay many multiples of the plan price, and the mean is dominated by that tail.

07

How the neighbours package it

Three live alternatives to the Squarespace shape, from public pricing and help pages checked in October and November 2026. Each one is a different answer to the same three questions: what burns credits, what happens at zero, and can you buy more.

Framer

Published burn rates, per-model pricing

  • A full AI landing page costs roughly 300 credits — the burn rate is published, so users can budget before they click.
  • Model choice is priced as a multiplier: 0.9× / 1× / 1.8× across its model tiers, passing the routing decision to the user.
  • The contrast with this model: Squarespace's build at 25 credits hides the same choice inside one blended rate.

Wix

Generation unmetered, agents metered

  • Site generation itself is unmetered; credits apply only to agentic tools that keep working after the site exists.
  • No rollover and no top-ups — the only way to get more capacity is to upgrade the plan.
  • In this simulator, that shape is the hard cap with top-ups switched away: margin is protected by construction, and heavy users become upgrade leads instead of cost centres.

Webflow

Pooled workspace credits, cheap refills

  • Credits are pooled at the workspace, not per site, so one busy site can draw on the whole pool.
  • Refill packs run 2,000 credits for $20 — one cent per credit, far under any plan's implied per-credit price in panel 1.
  • That price point only works if a Webflow credit buys far less compute per unit than a Squarespace credit does here — a reminder that credit counts mean nothing across vendors; cost per credit is the comparable figure.

—

Method, in one paragraph. Demand per user is drawn from a lognormal fitted to the median and p90 in panel 2. Each action draws a task from the mix, burns that task's credits, and costs what the rate card and routing mix say it costs. Users are simulated for six months so rollover banks settle, and month six is reported. Under a hard cap, actions stop at the credit balance; under overage, the full demand is served and the excess is billed; under top-ups, half the shortfall is bought and half is forgone. Figures are model outputs from these stated assumptions, not Squarespace's internal numbers.
